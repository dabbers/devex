Who this is for — and who it isn’t

A good fit today

  • You build small tools with AI and have nowhere to put them. The tool took an evening; the hosting, the sign-in and the backups are what killed it.
  • You want one form, survey or intake queue at its own address, with the submissions going somewhere you control.
  • You want to own the software and the data without becoming the person who operates a server.
  • You already pay for Claude, or don’t mind starting.

Not yet

  • You need a CRM or a help desk. Neither is built. Forms is the catalog.
  • Your changes are mostly a logo and a colour. Everything here goes through ask → preview → approve, which is more steps than a settings screen for small edits. The trade, in full.
  • It’s client-facing, or you’d genuinely miss it for a day. One machine, one operator, no second machine to fail over to.
  • You use ChatGPT, not Claude. The agent runs on a Claude subscription; nothing else drives it.
  • You need a spreadsheet out. Export is a JSON archive. There is no CSV.

The template is where you start, not what you get

Forms is a finished, working tool the day you install it, and plenty of people will use it exactly as it comes. But nothing in it is fixed. The wording, the fields, the look, who sees what, what happens after somebody answers — and what the app is fundamentally for. A form that collects enquiries can become the thing that sorts and scores them. Nobody is going to tell you that you have reached the edge of what the template allows, because there isn’t an edge.

That is the whole reason to start here rather than from nothing. Everybody who builds one of these builds the same sign-in, the same list with a search box, the same export button — work worth nothing to your business whether you pay for it in weekends, in a developer or in AI credits.

The honest limit is not the template. It is the agent: it does what it is told, as well as it can do it, and you see the result before anybody else does.

How a change happens

  1. You ask. “Add a budget-range dropdown to the enquiry form, and make it match my brand colours.” Plain sentences. It starts the moment you ask — there is no queue and no ticket, because the agent runs on your own subscription.
  2. It gets built somewhere else. A separate copy of the app, with a copy of your real records — a change tested against an empty app has not been tested. Automatic checks run, including something clicking through the pages the way a person would.
  3. You look at it. The new version gets a web address of its own, so you can click around it yourself. Your live site hasn’t moved.
  4. You approve it, or you don’t. Going live is a person’s decision, never the agent’s. A copy of everything is put aside first, and one click puts it back.

The trade, plainly. There is no settings screen, which is why nothing is off limits — and it is equally why changing your logo is a request rather than a click. Big changes come out well ahead on that deal. Four-second edits do not. If most of what you ever change is a colour and the address submissions go to, this is more steps than the settings page you left, and you should know that before you move.

What can go wrong

One machine, one person

There is nowhere else for your app to run if that machine has a bad day, and nobody else to pick up the phone. Fine for an internal form or a survey. Weigh it properly before you move something you would genuinely miss.

We can read your data

One operator with full access to the machine. Any claim otherwise would be theatre: scrambling the disk stops somebody who steals the hardware, it does not stop us, and a padlock drawn on a web page never has.

So can the agent

It has to read your records to check its own work against them. What it reads goes to Anthropic, under your subscription and your agreement with them rather than ours. If you can’t agree to that for a particular job, that job isn’t one to bring here yet.

Leaving takes one button

Export is a button inside the app — no request to make, nobody to ask, working from the first day. It gives you a JSON archive of everything the app holds. It is not a spreadsheet: there is no CSV export today.
