The hard part was never the app

A form builder, a contact list, a ticket queue — none of these are difficult software any more. Plenty of people have started one. Far fewer have one their team actually relies on, and the reason is never the part they set out to build.

What stops both of them is the same thing, and none of it is the thing anyone actually wanted. Somewhere for the information to live. A way to let the right people in and keep everyone else out. An address of its own. Copies, in case. Someone to notice when it stops working, and to know what to do about it. None of that is your business, and for most people it is not any part of their business.

So you rent the hosted version instead, and take the bill, the per-seat tax and somebody else’s roadmap along with it.

dabberz is the third option. Start from a template with the boring half already solved and proven, keep it as it is or change it by asking, and the operations stay ours. The software is yours and so is the data.

There is no settings page

Deliberately. Every tool like this eventually grows a configuration screen with four hundred checkboxes on it, because somebody had to guess in advance which things you might want to change — and whatever you actually want is never one of the four hundred.

So there isn’t one. Your colours, your logo, the wording, the questions, what happens after somebody answers them — you ask for all of it the same way, and it all goes through the steps below: checked, shown to you, live only when you say so. Nothing here is a setting, so nothing is off limits for not being one.

A template is where you start, not what you get

Every template is a finished, working tool on the day you install it. Plenty of people will use one exactly as it comes and never ask for anything. That is a perfectly good outcome and it costs you nothing but the install.

But it is a starting point, not a boundary. There is no part of it the agent can’t change — the wording, the look, the fields, what happens after someone fills it in, who sees what, what the thing is fundamentally for. A form that collects enquiries can become the thing that sorts and scores them. Nobody is going to tell you that you have reached the edge of what the template allows, because there isn’t one.

What this is really about is where your effort goes. Everybody who builds one of these builds the same sign-in, the same list with a search box, the same way of letting somebody out again — work that has been done thousands of times and is worth nothing to your business. Whether you would pay for that in weekends, in a developer, or in the hours and credits of asking an AI to do it for you, it is the same waste. Start from the part that is already solved, and spend what you have on the part that is actually yours.

Asking for a change

  1. Install it. You get your own copy at your own web address, with nothing shared with anybody else’s. About a minute.
  2. Say what you want changed. “Add a budget-range dropdown to the enquiry form, and make it match my brand colours.” An agent does the work on a separate machine, nowhere near your live site.
  3. Look at it. The change gets a web address of its own, so you can click around the new version yourself. Your live site hasn’t moved.
  4. It gets checked before you’re asked. The new version is tested automatically, including something clicking through the pages the way a person would, using a copy of your real information. Checking it against an empty app would prove nothing — the question is whether it still works with your things in it.
  5. You decide. Going live is a person’s decision, never the agent’s.
  6. Changed your mind? One click back to how it was. A copy of everything is put aside before any change goes live.

What it costs

Nothing today. Forms is free while dabberz is this early, there is no card on file, and there is no trial clock — a trial that expires once you’ve put real records into it is a trap, not an offer. You’ll get 90 days’ notice before that changes, and your data exports on the way out either way.

We would rather tell you the shape of the price before we’ve finished arguing about the number than show you a page with no number on it at all. You rent room to run things in — not seats, and not apps.

From $15 a month

Room for two to four tools. Run one busy one or four quiet ones; we don’t count them, and we don’t count the people using them. Try something, decide it was a bad idea, delete it — the bill doesn’t notice.

Outgrown it? Take more room

Roughly twice the room for roughly twice the price, and so on up. How we actually arrange that behind the scenes is our problem to solve, and never a line on your invoice.

Storage, past a point

The one thing we charge for by the amount, and we’d rather say so than hide it: “unlimited” storage on a flat fee is a promise that gets quietly broken two years later by a company that needs the money. So there is an allowance that most people will never reach, and past it you pay about what it costs us.

Your own Claude subscription

The agent runs on your own Claude subscription rather than ours, so the AI line on your dabberz bill is zero and cannot surprise you. Two things worth knowing rather than discovering: it is a separate subscription if you don’t already have one, and a long session of changes draws on your own usage limits, the same allowance you use for everything else.

What we won’t do

Charge per person

Per-seat pricing taxes collaboration. Adding a colleague so they can see one dashboard shouldn’t cost a full seat, so people share logins or leave somebody out. We charge on anything that isn’t a human being.

Bill you for growing

Nothing priced per tool, per record, per contact or per response. Each of those bills you more for doing well, on a number you never agreed to — and charging per tool would punish exactly the trying-things-out this is for. Storage past the allowance is the one exception, at roughly our cost, because that one is real weight on a real disk rather than a pricing idea.

Resell you AI

We take no cut of what the AI costs, and asking for changes is part of every tool rather than an upgrade — the opposite of “seats, and then extra for the AI ones”.

Make leaving hard

Every app exports everything it can import, from a button inside the app, with no request to make and nobody to ask. A platform for rescuing people from SaaS shouldn’t become the thing they were trying to leave.

Where your data goes

Step four says changes get checked against a copy of your real information. That is the part people ask about, so here is plainly what it means.

The copy is real, and short-lived

It is your actual information, not a made-up version — that is the whole point of checking against it. The copy is made when you ask for the change and deleted when the change goes live or gets thrown away.

The draft is as private as the real thing

The version you are shown sits at its own web address and asks people to sign in exactly the way your live one does. That address is readable rather than secret, on purpose: what keeps people out is the lock, not the hope that nobody finds the door.

The agent can read your information

It has to, to check its own work against it. So whatever it reads goes to Anthropic, under your subscription and your agreement with them rather than ours. If that isn’t something you can agree to for a particular job, then that job isn’t one to bring here yet, and we would rather say so than bury it.

So can we

One person, one machine, and full access to it. Any claim otherwise would be theatre. Scrambling the disk stops somebody who steals the hardware; it does not stop us, and a padlock drawn on a web page never has. Pick accordingly, and know that you can take everything and go at any point.

If you build things yourself

You will be able to bring your own, with one condition: it has to use the pieces the platform already provides for signing people in, storing things and sending mail. Those shared pieces are the only reason we can look after something without knowing what it does. Anything that brings its own way of signing people in is not something we can run this way.

Today, the route that works is the other end of the same deal: take a template and change it until it is yours. Most people will never need the first one.

Honest about where this is

That is fine for a survey or an internal form. Weigh it properly before you move something you would genuinely miss.
