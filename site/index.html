SaaS-as-a-service

The self-host for people who aren’t technical.

Install your own CRM, help desk or forms tool. We run it — the machine, the database, the backups, the uptime. When you want it changed, you ask in plain English, see the change running somewhere safe, and decide whether it goes live.

The question was never “can I run my own tools?” It was “do I want to be the person who runs them?”

Request access How it works

You own it. You don’t run it.

Self-hosting solves the bill and hands you a second job: patching, monitoring, restoring, and finding out at 2am that nobody noticed it was down. Buying SaaS solves the second job and hands back the bill, the per-seat tax, and somebody else’s roadmap.

dabberz is the third option. The software is yours and so is the data. The operations are ours.

Asking for a change

  1. Install an app. It gets its own database, its own address, its own copy of the code. About a minute.
  2. Say what you want changed. “Add a company field to the contact form.” An agent does the work — on a branch, on its own machine, against a copy of your real data taken a moment earlier.
  3. Look at it. The change gets a preview address of its own. Your live site has not moved.
  4. It gets checked. The app’s own tests run, and a real browser opens the preview to confirm the page still loads and renders.
  5. You decide. Promotion is a person’s decision, never the agent’s.
  6. Changed your mind? One click back to the previous version. The database is snapshotted before anything ships.

What we won’t do

Charge per person

Per-seat pricing taxes collaboration. Adding a colleague so they can see one dashboard shouldn’t cost a full seat, so people share logins or leave somebody out. We charge on anything that isn’t a human being.

Charge per gigabyte

The replacement everyone reaches for, and it’s the same tax wearing different clothes: a bill that grows with your own success, on an axis you didn’t choose. Nobody should learn to delete things to control a bill.

Resell you AI

You bring your own Claude subscription. We don’t mark up tokens, and agent access is included in every app rather than sold as an upgrade — the opposite of “seats, plus extra for agents”.

Make leaving hard

Every app exports everything it can import. A platform for rescuing people from SaaS shouldn’t become the thing they were trying to leave.

Honest about where this is

dabberz is early. One app is in the catalog today — Forms — and the platform runs on a single machine operated by one person. The code is public, including the parts that aren’t finished. If that’s interesting rather than disqualifying, get in touch.
